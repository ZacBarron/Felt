# Felt voice and style

How Felt talks. Every screen, button, notification, email and lesson follows this guide. When this guide and older copy disagree, this guide wins.

## Who we are talking to

A poker player on their phone, often between hands, on a break or on the way to the game. They are smart and busy. Some are new to study; some have played for years. They want to get better at the table, and they can tell when they are being sold to.

## How we sound

Felt sounds like a good coach sitting next to you. **Calm, direct and warm.** We say what is true and what to do next.

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Clear | Clever |
| Confident | Loud or hyped |
| Warm | Cute or chummy |
| Honest about what we know | Vague, or sure of things we cannot be sure of |
| Encouraging about skill | Guilt trips about time or streaks |

## The rules

### 1. One idea per sentence

Write short sentences. Aim for under 15 words. Never go over 20 words on a screen.

- **Yes:** Learn the math and the strategy. Practice a few minutes a day.
- **No:** Exact math, solver based patterns and player tendencies, each labelled for how sure it is.

### 2. Go easy on commas

Use at most one comma in a sentence on a screen. Do not pack a list of three or more into a sentence. Use a short list, or split it into sentences.

- **Yes:** Three range decisions. Two lesson questions. Same for everyone today.
- **No:** Three range decisions, two lesson questions, rated, timed and shareable.

### 3. Write whole sentences in body text

Body text uses full sentences with a verb. Titles and labels can be short phrases.

- **Yes:** It takes a few minutes a day.
- **No:** A few minutes a day.

### 4. Lead with the player

Say what the player gets or does, in their words. Put the useful part first.

- **Yes:** Skip lessons you already know.
- **No:** Our placement system marks lessons as tested out.

### 5. Buttons say what happens

Start with a verb. The label should make sense on its own, out of context.

- **Yes:** Start lesson. Save backup. Email me a code.
- **No:** Continue. OK. Submit. Click here.

"Next" and "Done" are fine only when the screen makes the action obvious, as in a step by step flow.

### 6. Plain words, real poker words

Poker terms are welcome. Players know them: 3bet, button, pot odds, c-bet, river. System and research jargon is not welcome.

| Say | Not |
| --- | --- |
| still settling | provisional |
| fitted from everyone's answers | calibrated |
| mixed together | interleaved |
| our estimate | model output |
| how much your results swing | standard deviation |
| close calls | boundary hands, edge hands |

When a newcomer might not know a poker abbreviation, spell it out the first time on that screen: "VPIP (how often you put money in)".

### 7. Honest numbers

- Give every number its unit: 5 min, 12 bb/100, $14.99 a month.
- Say when a number is an estimate: "about", "our estimate". Do not hide uncertainty in symbols.
- Say which parts are arithmetic and which are estimates (see ENGAGEMENT.md).
- Never round in the player's favor to make them feel good.

### 8. Encourage skill, never pressure

- Praise what the player did, not the player. "Right. That hand folds 90% of the time."
- Mistakes are information. "Not quite" with the reason, never "Wrong!".
- No guilt, no countdowns, no fake urgency, no "Don't break your streak!".
- Reminders say what is waiting. "Your review is ready." Not "We miss you."

### 9. Accessible by default

- Write at about a grade 6 to 8 reading level on screens. Lessons can go deeper, but each sentence still carries one idea.
- Never carry meaning only in punctuation, symbols, color or position. "~1264" also needs words nearby ("still settling").
- Link and button text must make sense when read alone by a screen reader.
- Avoid semicolons and colons in body text. Use two sentences instead.
- No em dashes or en dashes as punctuation. Ranges like "10 to 15" use words.
- Avoid ALL CAPS for meaning. Small uppercase section labels are styling only.
- Use numerals for numbers (3 lessons, not three lessons) except to start a sentence.

## Mechanics

- **Sentence case** for everything: titles, buttons, tabs, labels. "Daily challenge", not "Daily Challenge".
- **Felt** and **Felt Pro** and **Felt Rating** are always capitalized.
- **Contractions** are welcome when they sound natural: you're, it's, don't.
- **You and your** for the player. **We** only for Felt as a team, sparingly.
- **Numbers:** 9-handed, 6-max, 100bb, bb/100, 2.5bb. Money with its currency symbol.
- **Dates:** Oct 19, not 10/19. Times: 7:30 pm.
- **Periods:** none on buttons, labels, titles or single short fragments in lists. Use them on full sentences.
- **Exclamation marks:** almost never. Earn one with something real, like finishing a whole track.
- **Emoji:** never in the interface. The daily share grid uses colored squares on purpose.

## Before you ship copy

Read it out loud. If you run out of breath, split the sentence. If a friend would ask "meaning what?", rewrite it. Then check:

- [ ] Each sentence has one idea and fewer than 20 words.
- [ ] No sentence on a screen has more than one comma.
- [ ] Buttons start with a verb and make sense alone.
- [ ] Every number has a unit, and estimates say so.
- [ ] No jargon from the "Not" column above.
- [ ] No em dashes, semicolons or exclamation marks.
- [ ] Terms match the glossary.
